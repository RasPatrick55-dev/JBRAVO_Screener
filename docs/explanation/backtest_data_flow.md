# Backtest Data Flow

## Overview
`scripts/backtest.py` runs the JBRAVO strategy backtest as part of the pipeline,
consuming the latest screened candidates and daily bars to generate per-symbol
performance metrics for downstream analytics and dashboards.

## Inputs
- **screener_candidates (PostgreSQL)**: latest run_date used to select symbols.
- **daily_bars (PostgreSQL)**: historical OHLCV; history depth is controlled by
  `BACKTEST_LOOKBACK_DAYS` and `BACKTEST_MIN_HISTORY_BARS`.
- **Alpaca Market Data API**: IEX primary with optional SIP fallback for backfill.

## Process
- Loads bars up to the run_date; if history is short, backfills from the API and
  upserts into `daily_bars`.
- Uses a custom backtester (Backtrader-style) to simulate the JBRAVO strategy.
- Computes the existing composite score and ranks available symbols without
  changing the ranking formula. This portfolio simulator does not independently
  enforce the live screener's full candidate-gate contract.
- Queues entries and enabled close-derived exits under the causal fill contract
  below. Invalid symbols or short-history symbols are skipped before simulation.

## Causal daily-bar fill contract

This is a long-only OHLC simulation, not an observed execution sequence or a
change to the paper executor/monitor.

1. **Signals and entries.** Rank a symbol only after its daily bar completes.
   Queue at most `top_n` new entries, reserving `max_positions` capacity including
   pending entries. Attempt each entry at that symbol's next available bar's
   **open**, using cash then available and the existing allocation/whole-share
   sizing. Initial ATR protection uses the signal bar's ATR and the fill price;
   neither the fill bar's high nor its completed indicators are available yet.
   An unaffordable or capacity-blocked attempt is discarded; a later completed
   bar can generate a new signal. There is no signal expiry across missing bars.
2. **Opening precedence.** For existing longs, an open at/below an active stop
   liquidates at the open (a gap never fills at the better stop price). Otherwise
   a previously queued full exit fills at the open; otherwise a queued partial
   sells half the shares, rounded down, with at least one share retained. Opening
   exits precede new entries. Multiple opening actions follow existing insertion
   order; the simulator makes no simultaneous-allocation or liquidity claim.
3. **Protective stops.** Test remaining positions, including entries filled at
   this open, against their existing stop levels before any close-derived
   adjustment. The highest active long stop governs; ATR wins an exact tie with
   the trailing stop. If the low reaches it without an opening gap, assume a
   stop-level fill, subject to the existing slippage/cost cash model.
4. **Completed information.** Only surviving positions may incorporate this
   bar's high/close into trailing/ATR adjustments, effective on the next bar.
   EMA/MACD/other enabled exits and maximum-hold conditions queue full exits for
   the next available open. A full-exit signal suppresses a competing partial
   signal; a partial-only signal also waits for the next available open. Partial
   fills do not tighten a stop until subsequent completed-bar processing.
5. **OHLC ambiguity.** Daily bars cannot establish whether a high or low happened
   first. Conservatively, a touched existing stop wins over this bar's closing
   signals, and the bar's high is not credited as pre-stop favorable excursion.
   A queued partial can fill at the open followed by a stop of the remainder;
   this uses only the known opening event and old stop, not an invented intrabar
   sequence. Actual stop liquidity, spreads and fills remain unmodeled.
6. **Missing/end bars.** A missing symbol bar defers its pending actions and
   cannot generate a stop fill. Equity carries its last observed close, a stale
   mark rather than proof of executable value. Holding time remains calendar
   days and is assessed when a symbol bar is available. End-of-data signals stay
   pending, positions remain marked, and no final liquidation is invented.
   A present bar with missing, nonfinite, nonpositive or inconsistent OHLC fails
   explicitly; the close is never substituted for an unavailable open.

This contract does not fix point-in-time universe selection, transaction-cost
calibration or out-of-sample provenance. Default costs remain zero. No historical
results are regenerated by this change.

## Cost accounting contract

- Preserve existing cash fills: entry cash debit is `qty * price * (1 + slippage)
  + trade_cost`; each executed exit credits `qty * price * (1 - slippage)
  - trade_cost`. Prices remain the causal model's reference prices, not separate
  observed execution prices. Cost parameters/defaults and sizing are unchanged.
- `Trade.gross_pnl` is `(exit_price - entry_price) * exited_qty`, before costs.
  `Trade.net_pnl` deducts allocated entry fee/slippage and this exit's fee/slippage.
  **`Trade.pnl` now aliases the net result**, including in result/export data;
  consumers needing the old gross meaning must select `gross_pnl` explicitly.
  `entry_fee`, `entry_slippage`, `exit_fee` and `exit_slippage` expose the ledger.
  Slippage amounts are derived from actual cash debit/credit arithmetic.
- Entry costs are recorded once and retained on the open position. A partial
  exit receives costs in proportion to exited shares versus remaining shares;
  those allocations are subtracted from remaining entry costs. The final exit
  takes all remaining costs, including floating-point allocation residuals.
  Each executed partial/final exit pays one exit fee. Skipped entries and skipped
  single-share partials pay nothing. No ledger value is rounded before posting.
- `Position.cost_basis` includes reference-price principal plus remaining entry
  costs. With absolute tolerance `1e-9` currency units for the synthetic fixtures:
  `cash = initial_cash + sum(realized net_pnl) - sum(open cost_basis)` and
  `equity = cash + sum(open qty * last_observed_close)` (entry price before a mark).
  Realized net P&L excludes unallocated open-position costs and unrealized value.
  `Unrealized P&L` is marked open value minus remaining basis; it reconciles with
  realized net P&L to total marked equity change. Missing-bar marks can be stale.
- Simulator win rate/profit factor and caller per-symbol/exit-reason statistics
  use net exits. Exit-reason `avg_pnl`/`total_pnl` retain their names but mean net.
  Equity return/drawdown metrics already include costs and unrealized marks;
  `Realized Net P&L`, `Remaining Cost Basis` and `Unrealized P&L` report them apart.
  MFE, `exit_pct` and exit efficiency remain reference-price diagnostics, not net
  returns; trade-return ratios use net P&L over reference-price share exposure.
- Simulator and per-symbol profit factor divide positive net gains by absolute
  negative net losses. With zero aggregate losses, positive gains give infinity;
  break-even-only exits and no completed trades give zero. Break-even exits do
  not contribute to either sum. These conventions do not imply profitability.
- Per-symbol `max_drawdown` starts its cumulative realized net P&L curve at zero,
  so an initial loss and an all-loss sequence count in full. It is the most
  negative decline from the realized curve's running peak, in currency units.
  It differs from simulator `Max Drawdown`, a percentage of marked portfolio
  equity that includes open positions. Separate consumers retain their own
  metric formulas; this packet changes these simulator/caller calculations only.
- Local `metrics.calculate_metrics`, trade-performance and dashboard CSV helpers
  prefer `pnl`; its net alias prevents those consumers from selecting gross when
  both fields exist. Weekly summaries and DB backtest inserts already consume
  `net_pnl`. No consumer outside this packet changes. Historical CSVs are not
  migrated or retrospectively relabelled, and paper/live accounting is unchanged.

## Outputs
- `PortfolioBacktester.results()` retains every `Trade` field in declaration
  order, including cost fields, even when no trades complete. `run_backtest` uses
  the simulator's genuine `net_pnl` without replacing it with gross and reaches
  zero-trade per-symbol summaries and enabled export boundaries; pending terminal
  entries/exits remain pending, without forced liquidation.
- **DB**: inserts into `backtest_results` with full per-symbol metrics.
- **CSV**: optional and non-authoritative; CSV export is disabled when DB is enabled.

## Caller and CLI execution contract

`run_backtest(..., trade_cost=0.0, slippage=0.0)` and the normal CLI expose
`--trade-cost` and `--slippage`. The fee is currency per executed fill and must
be finite and nonnegative; slippage is an adverse fractional price adjustment
and must be finite in `[0, 1)` (`0.001` means 10 basis points per side). Boolean,
missing and nonnumeric caller values are rejected. Valid values reach the
simulator unchanged and are logged as `BACKTEST_COSTS`. Both legacy defaults
remain zero; they are not calibrated execution costs. No cost configuration,
strategy parameter, ranking formula or paper executor changes.

A valid simulation with evaluated symbols and zero completed trades succeeds,
including pending terminal entries/exits. A caller with no usable evaluated
symbol raises `ValueError`; invalid/missing/short-history symbols may still be
skipped when other symbols are evaluated. Existing result counts (`tested`,
`skipped`) are retained. Simulator instances retain their equity, open-position
basis and pending-action dictionaries; the caller return remains a count dict.

Simulation and filesystem export exceptions propagate from the caller.
Requested database output is mandatory: a false insert result or exception
also fails. The CLI returns 1 on simulation/output failure, 2 for unavailable
mandatory DB/candidates or an invalid run date, and argparse returns 2 for
invalid cost arguments. Empty candidates are an unsuccessful unevaluated run,
not an ordinary zero-trade result. Connections are closed after simulation.
Successful CLI runs remain DB-first with CSV export disabled, including the
deprecated `--export-csv` flag. Direct callers can enable temporary CSV exports;
the equity CSV retains its existing `date,equity` schema.

### Offline execution-path coverage

`tests/test_backtest_execution_path.py` imports the actual module and exercises
the actual caller and `main`, without AST extraction. It replaces environment/
credential loading, logger placement, provider SDK and DB connection boundaries
before import; the top-level `utils` boundary package initialization is bypassed.
Config loading executes from the checkout. The import's data-directory creation
is redirected and caller exports/logs are confined to pytest temporary storage.
The process environment excludes provider/DB configuration; tests block sockets,
provider clients, subprocesses and writes outside temporary storage.

Actual SQL construction/row normalization operates against inert cursor
responses, never a database. Real indicator computation, composite scoring,
alignment, simulator execution, summaries and the unchanged `scripts.utils`
filesystem CSV writer run on synthetic bars. Checks include nonzero parameter
delivery, default/invalid costs, prefix invariance of all indicator/score columns
and queued entry signals when future bars are appended, date clipping, empty/
nonempty exports, marked equity/open basis/pending actions, no evaluated symbols,
simulation errors, mandatory DB-output errors and a real temporary-filesystem
export failure. Prefix invariance is demonstrated for these fixtures only.

Run in an isolated process with `PYTEST_DISABLE_PLUGIN_AUTOLOAD=1` and
`PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1`:
`python -B -m pytest --noconftest -p no:cacheprovider
tests/test_backtest_execution_path.py tests/test_backtest_causal_fills.py
tests/test_backtest_cost_accounting.py -q`.

This does not qualify dotenv/credential discovery, real SDK construction,
network/DB connectivity, real SQL schemas/transactions, logger rotation, the
`python -m` launcher, full pipeline orchestration, production export storage,
recovery from partial exports, all consumers, historical universe/data
provenance, calibrated costs or profitability. The CLI still requires a DB;
these doubles do not create an offline production mode. Older DB I/O tests
include deprecated CSV-fallback expectations and are not covered by this packet.

## Logging
- Per-symbol fetch status:
  - `[INFO] BARS_LOADED_FROM_DB symbol=... rows=...`
  - `[INFO] BACKTEST_BARS_BACKFILL symbol=... rows=...`
  - `[WARN] BAR_CACHE_MISS symbol=... reason=<missing_bars|insufficient_bars>`

## Validation
- Offline causal-contract unit tests use synthetic in-memory bars and compile
  the actual simulator definitions without the module's environment loading,
  provider/database imports or logging initialization. With
  `PYTEST_DISABLE_PLUGIN_AUTOLOAD=1` set in the test process environment, run:
  `python -B -m pytest --noconftest -p no:cacheprovider tests/test_backtest_causal_fills.py`.
  These tests cover timing, stop precedence/gaps, partials, missing bars, invalid
  OHLC and cash/position invariants. Zero-trade regressions also compile the actual
  `run_backtest` caller and exercise its schema, summary and export/DB branches
  using mocked bar loading, indicator preparation, DB writes and CSV exports.
  They do not test module initialization, CLI/pipeline wiring, real indicators,
  provider/database connectivity or filesystem export behavior, and establish
  no performance or operational acceptance. The following data-flow checks
  require separate runtime authority:
- Symbols processed match latest `screener_candidates` with >= `BACKTEST_MIN_HISTORY_BARS` bars.
- API backfill grows `daily_bars` when new symbols appear.

Offline cost regressions in `tests/test_backtest_cost_accounting.py` use the same
isolated definitions and mocked boundaries. Run both test files with the command
above plus `tests/test_backtest_cost_accounting.py`. They cover zero/nonzero costs,
even/odd partials, final allocation residuals, skipped fills, multiple symbols,
open positions, empty schemas, and actual caller net summary/export data. A
Decimal calculation independently checks a nine-share partial/final ledger.
The nonzero-cost caller test now supplies explicit caller parameters through a
passthrough constructor capture; normal caller defaults remain zero, not calibrated costs.
The real `metrics.calculate_metrics` function is also compiled in isolation to
check its P&L-first compatibility; complete consumer initialization is untested.

## Compatibility
- Output is ready for `scripts/metrics.py` and dashboard ingestion.
- DB is the source of truth; CSV exports are for ad-hoc debugging only.
